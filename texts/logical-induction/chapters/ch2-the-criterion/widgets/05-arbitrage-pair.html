The arbitrage pair: profiting from a tautology no one's proven yet

Sources for this widget

  • §3.2 "Deductive Processes" — for the propositional-consistency framing where syntactically distinct prime sentences can be assigned independently, even when one is the negation of the other.

Take two sentences whose disjunction is provable, but neither of which individually is. The cleanest example: $\varphi := \quot{\text{RH}}$ and $\psi := \quot{\lnot\text{RH}}$ (the Riemann hypothesis and its negation). $\varphi \lor \psi$ is provable instantly (excluded middle); neither disjunct is — the Riemann hypothesis remains open.

A trader buys 10 shares of $\varphi$ at 20¢ and 10 shares of $\psi$ at 30¢. Until $\bar{D}$ proves the disjunction, this looks dicey. Afterwards, every p.c. world values the portfolio at $\ge +\$5$ — and the trader keeps doing it every day.

The toy

Two prime sentences $\varphi, \psi$ — treated as atomic, even though semantically they are negations of each other. A p.c. world can assign $(\varphi, \psi) \in \{0, 1\}^2$ — four worlds in total:

  • $W_{00}$: $\varphi = 0, \psi = 0$ — neither is true. (Logically incoherent, but propositionally consistent until $\bar{D}$ rules it out.)
  • $W_{01}$: $\varphi = 0, \psi = 1$ — RH is false.
  • $W_{10}$: $\varphi = 1, \psi = 0$ — RH is true.
  • $W_{11}$: $\varphi = 1, \psi = 1$ — both true. (Logically incoherent, but again propositionally consistent.)

The deductive process emits the tautology $\varphi \lor \psi$ on day $t^\star$ (you set this with the slider). That single theorem rules out $W_{00}$, leaving the three worlds where at least one of $\varphi, \psi$ is true.

Why this is striking

This trader never buys a sentence that $\bar{D}$ resolves. The Riemann hypothesis itself stays open forever — neither $\varphi$ nor $\psi$ enters $\bar{D}_\infty$. And yet the trader extracts unbounded profit, simply because the disjunction is provable. The §3.5 definition of exploitation has to count this as exploitation — otherwise the criterion would have nothing to say about traders who arbitrage between unresolved sentences, and the LI would be free to assign incoherent prices.

This is also why §4.1 ("Convergence and Coherence") gets the limit-coherence theorem essentially for free: any market that fails to respect provable disjunctions is exploitable by exactly this construction.

"Once $\bar{D}$ says $(\varphi \lor \psi)$, all remaining p.c. worlds will agree that the portfolio $-5 + 10\varphi + 10\psi$ has a value of at least $+5$, despite the fact that neither $\varphi$ nor $\psi$ is ever proven." — Garrabrant et al. (2016), §3.5

Why $W_{00}$ and $W_{11}$ are p.c. Definition 3.2.3 requires $W$ to respect Boolean structure on Boolean combinations of primes. If $\varphi$ and $\psi$ are syntactically distinct prime sentences (e.g., distinct first-order formulas in the language), $W$ is free to assign them independently. The semantic fact that $\psi \equiv \lnot \varphi$ is a theorem $\bar{D}$ has to actually emit before $W_{00}$ and $W_{11}$ get ruled out. In a more concrete setting, $\varphi$ and $\psi$ might be two formally distinct ways of expressing related facts — say, "for all $x$, $P(x)$" and "there exists $x$ such that $\lnot P(x)$" — whose disjunction is provable in PA but which don't have a one-line propositional reduction to each other.
