The 17 desiderata: which combinations are achievable?

Logical Induction companion · widget 2 of the Ch 1 batch on Garrabrant et al. (2016) §1.1

Sources for this widget
  • Garrabrant et al. (2016) §1.1 "Desiderata for Reasoning under Logical Uncertainty" — the verbatim list of seventeen, plus the closing impossibility paragraph (p. 9).
  • Sawin & Demski (2013), Non-Omniscience, Probabilistic Inference, and Metamathematics, §6 — the impossibility of $\{$Computable, Non-Dogmatism, Gaifman, weak Coherence$\}$ in combination. (The paper-level citation in Garrabrant et al. is "Sawin 2013, $\Pi_1$$\Pi_2$".)
  • §1.1 closing paragraph — "Desiderata 1, 2, and 13 cannot be satisfied simultaneously"; LI achieves desiderata 1 through 12, and explicitly fails 13, 14, 15.
  • Background reading: Demski, "An Intuitive Guide to Logical Induction" — the "what would we want?" framing.

Section 1.1 of the paper lists seventeen properties that have been proposed as desirable for a reasoner facing logical uncertainty. Some are mild ("be computable"); others are heroic ("dominate the universal semimeasure"). They are not independent — several known results say certain combinations are impossible.

The single-desideratum subsets are all trivially achievable (the constant-zero or constant-one belief state usually does the job, modulo computability). Interesting impossibilities only emerge from pairs and triples.

Which combinations are known to be impossible?

    These are the only impossibility tuples this widget checks. Section 1.1 mentions exactly these; further impossibility results in the LI literature (e.g., on self-trust + self-knowledge, or on coherence + self-reference) live in later sections and other papers, and are out of scope here.

    What does LI itself achieve?

    Per the closing paragraph of §1.1: the LI algorithm of §5 satisfies desiderata 1 through 12 (Computable Approximability through Approximate Inexploitability). It explicitly fails Gaifman Inductivity (13), Efficiency (14), and Decision Rationality (15) — the first by Sawin's incompatibility, the latter two because LI is purely inductive and pays no attention to the cost of thinking. Counterpossibles (16) and Old Evidence (17) are listed by Garrabrant et al. as "interesting but unclear".

    "Our algorithm does satisfy desiderata 1 through 12. In fact, our algorithm is designed to meet only $\{$1$\}$ and $\{$12$\}$, from which 2–11 will all be shown to follow." — Garrabrant et al. (2016), §1.1
    Why this matters for the rest of the paper

    The point of §1.1 is not to enumerate trivia. It frames the paper's central design choice: LI is built around just two desiderata — Computable Approximability (#1) and Approximate Inexploitability (#12) — and the other ten that LI achieves are theorems derived from those two. That mirrors how classical probability theory is built around a single criterion (don't be Dutch-bookable) from which the Kolmogorov axioms follow. So the seemingly-arbitrary list of 17 is doing real work: it tells you which properties the authors thought were worth building around (1, 12), which are bonuses (2–11), and which have to be given up (13, 14, 15).

    How this widget thinks about achievability. A subset is reported "achievable" when it contains no known incompatibility tuple — but absence of a known impossibility is not proof of joint achievability. The space of 17 desiderata has $2^{17} = 131{,}072$ subsets, and the open-questions section of the paper makes clear that several joint achievability questions are not settled. Treat green verdicts as "no known obstruction" rather than "constructed". The two impossibility tuples checked here are exactly those named in §1.1's closing paragraph; the LI's twelve achievements are exactly those claimed (and proved later in the paper). For the formal definitions of each desideratum, see the linked paragraphs in §1.1.
