Theorem 4.1.2 (Limit Coherence). The limit $\mathbb{P}_\infty$ of a logical inductor over $\mathcal{T}$ defines a coherent probability measure on the consistent-completion worlds of $\mathcal{T}$.

Per Gaifman (1964), to be a coherent probability measure $\mathbb{P}_\infty$ must satisfy three conditions. Each fails $\Rightarrow$ a specific trader exploits.

  • (a) If $\mathcal{T} \vdash \varphi$, then $\mathbb{P}_\infty(\varphi) = 1$.
  • (b) If $\mathcal{T} \vdash \lnot \varphi$, then $\mathbb{P}_\infty(\varphi) = 0$.
  • (c) If $\mathcal{T} \vdash \lnot(\varphi \land \psi)$, then $\mathbb{P}_\infty(\varphi \lor \psi) = \mathbb{P}_\infty(\varphi) + \mathbb{P}_\infty(\psi)$.

What this proves

Why the three together suffice

Gaifman (1964) showed that any function on sentences satisfying these three conditions extends to a probability measure on the consistent-completion worlds of $\mathcal{T}$. So:

  • $\mathbb{P}_\infty$ assigns probability 1 to provable sentences ((a)).
  • $\mathbb{P}_\infty$ assigns probability 0 to refutable sentences ((b)).
  • $\mathbb{P}_\infty$ is finitely additive on disjoint events ((c)).

The limit beliefs of an LI form a probability distribution over the ways $\mathcal{T}$ could be completed — exactly what classical Bayesianism demands for empirical uncertainty, but extended to logical uncertainty.

"$\mathbb{P}_\infty$ is coherent, i.e., it gives rise to an internally consistent probability measure $\overline{\mathbb{P}}$ on the set $\mathrm{C}(\mathcal{T})$ of all worlds consistent with $\mathcal{T}$." — Garrabrant et al. (2016), §4.1 Theorem 4.1.2

Three exploits, one template. All three traders are buy-low/sell-high variants. (a) buys $\varphi$ at $1 - \varepsilon$ once $\bar{D}$ proves it (worth $1$ in every remaining p.c. world). (b) sells $\varphi$ at $\varepsilon$ once $\bar{D}$ proves $\lnot\varphi$ (worth $0$ in every remaining world). (c) executes the three-leg arbitrage: sell $\varphi \lor \psi$ at $\mathbb{P}_\infty(\varphi) + \mathbb{P}_\infty(\psi) - \varepsilon$, buy $\varphi$ and $\psi$ at their stated prices, then wait for $\bar{D}$ to prove $\lnot(\varphi \land \psi)$. Once that arrives, the portfolio's value is $-(\varphi \lor \psi) + \varphi + \psi = 0$ in every remaining p.c. world (because $\varphi \land \psi$ is excluded), so the trader pockets the $\varepsilon$ price gap. Same template, same conclusion: violation of any Gaifman condition is exploitable, so an LI's limit must satisfy all three. The full proof handles continuity (the actual traders ramp through prices rather than using strict thresholds) and timing of the "$\bar{D}$ proves" event.
