Theorem 4.3.1 (Learning Pseudorandom Frequencies). If $(\varphi_n)$ is an EC sequence of decidable sentences pseudorandom with frequency $p$ (no polynomial-time predictor can do better than $p$), then $\mathbb{P}_n(\varphi_n) \to p$ in a timely manner.

The Ackermann-digit example: $\varphi_n := \quot{\pi[\mathrm{Ack}(n, n)] = 7}$. Each individual sentence has a determinate truth value (it's decidable in principle), but $\mathrm{Ack}(n, n)$ grows non-elementarily and no polynomial-time predictor can compute $\pi[\mathrm{Ack}(n, n)]$. The LI learns to assign $\approx 0.1$ to each $\varphi_n$ — long before any one of them is resolved.

Predict, then reveal

Each $\varphi_n$ has a determinate truth value. What is the limit $\lim_{n \to \infty} \mathbb{P}_n(\varphi_n)$ for a logical inductor over $\mathsf{PA}$?

Why this is not a contradiction with Provability Induction

Provability Induction (Theorem 4.2.1) requires the limiting probabilities to themselves be efficiently computable. For the Ackermann-digit sequence, the per-sentence limit probabilities form a sequence in $\{0, 1\}^\mathbb{N}$ that is not efficiently computable (it depends on $\pi$ digits at non-elementary positions). So Provability Induction has nothing to say about the diagonal $\mathbb{P}_n(\varphi_n)$.

What §4.3 establishes is a weaker statement: even when the per-sentence pattern is uncomputable, if the frequency of truth is $p$ on every polynomial-time-extracted subsequence, the LI learns to predict at frequency $p$. The Ackermann-digit sequence has frequency $1/10$ (modulo the Borel-normality assumption on $\pi$), so the LI converges to $0.1$.

"Assuming there's no good way to predict the Ackermann digits of $\pi$ using a $\bar{\mathbb{P}}$-generable divergent weighting, $\mathbb{P}$ will assign probability 10% to each $\varphi^{\text{Ack}}_n$ in a timely manner, while it waits for the resources to determine whether the sentence is true or false." — Garrabrant et al. (2016), §4.3

What this widget simulates and what it can't. Each $\varphi_n$ is given a truth value sampled iid Bernoulli($p$) at startup (or on "reroll"); this is a stand-in for the real Ackermann-digit-of-$\pi$ truth values, which the user's browser cannot compute in finite time. The "Bayesian forced to ground truth" line displays those truth values directly. The "LI" line is a stylised trajectory: Brownian-style noise around $p$ that decays as $n$ grows, modelling timely-manner convergence. The actual LIA's price for $\varphi_n$ is more nuanced — see Flint's uniform_digits_of_pi.py for a runnable LIA-toy that produces a real (non-stylised) convergence trajectory on a related setting. The qualitative claim — $\mathbb{P}_n(\varphi_n) \to p$ even though individual truth values are 0 or 1 — is the content of Theorem 4.3.1; the widget renders that claim, not a faithful LIA simulation.
