Theorem 4.2.1 (Provability Induction). Let $(\varphi_n)$ be an efficiently computable sequence of theorems of $\mathcal{T}$. Then $\mathbb{P}_n(\varphi_n) \to 1$ in a timely manner.

The Ramanujan/Hardy analogy: $\varphi_n$ is the $n$-th of Ramanujan's untrustworthy-looking formulas. The deductive process $\bar{D}$ (Hardy's verification) is slow — proving $\varphi_n$ takes $f(n)$ days, where $f$ is fast-growing. Without an inductor, Hardy's belief in $\varphi_n$ jumps from "no opinion" to certainty only on day $f(n)$. With an inductor, Hardy starts trusting Ramanujan: $\mathbb{P}_n(\varphi_n) \approx 1$ for all $n \ge N^\star$, regardless of how fast $f$ grows.

Why this is striking

$\bar{D}$ has a runtime budget that's polynomial in $n$ (we restrict to characters-of-proof up to $n$). $f(n)$ — the actual proof length — is allowed to grow as fast as we like, even non-elementarily. Yet the LI's belief in $\varphi_n$ is right by day $n$. The gap $f(n) - n$ between LI-recognition and deductive-confirmation grows without bound; the LI is trusting Ramanujan based on inductive pattern-matching, not on completing the underlying proofs.

The exploitation argument behind the proof is an "if not, then a trader earns $\varepsilon$ infinitely often": if $\mathbb{P}_n(\varphi_n)$ stays below $1 - \varepsilon$ infinitely often, a trader buys $\varphi_n$ at the low price each time and waits for $\bar{D}$ to catch up. Once it does, the share is worth $1$ in every remaining p.c. world. That nets $\approx \varepsilon$ infinitely often off a finite stake — the §3.5 exploitation pattern. So the LI's belief must converge to $1$.

"$\mathbb{P}$ will learn to start believing $\varphi_n$ by day $n$ at the latest, despite the fact that $\varphi_n$ won't be deductively confirmed until day $f(n)$." — Garrabrant et al. (2016), §4.2

What this widget is and isn't. The "Hardy with logical inductor" panel uses a stylised pattern: $\mathbb{P}_d(\varphi_n) \approx 1$ for $d \ge \max(n, N^\star)$ where $N^\star$ is a fixed "warm-up" parameter. The actual LI's behaviour is more nuanced: prices may rise gradually rather than snapping, and the warm-up $N^\star$ depends on the trader who eventually catches the pattern. The shape that's guaranteed by the theorem is "$\mathbb{P}_n(\varphi_n) \to 1$"; the exact trajectory is implementation-dependent. Treat the heatmap as cartoon showing the key qualitative win — the LI's diagonal $\mathbb{P}_n(\varphi_n)$ is green by some finite $n$, regardless of $f$ — rather than a faithful simulation of the §5 LIA. The "Hardy without inductor" panel is the strict-no-inductor reasoner: 0 until $\bar{D}$ proves the sentence, then 1. This is also a cartoon (a fancier non-LI reasoner could do better), but it captures the idea that without inductive pattern-matching there's no reason to be confident before the proof finishes.
