SAT or UNSAT?

NN Verification · Chapter 4 · companion to Albarghouthi (2021) §4.1–§4.2.

Each round below is a formula in linear real arithmetic over two variables $x_1, x_2$. Each atom is a closed half-space — its interior shaded on the plane below. A conjunction is the intersection of half-spaces; a disjunction is their union. Decide whether the formula is satisfiable. If it is, drag the point onto a satisfying assignment. If it isn't, press Declare UNSAT. This is the same yes/no the SMT solver in Ch 6 will be asked about an encoded neural network — a model is a counterexample, UNSAT means the property is proved.

Half-space arrangement on $\mathbb{R}^2$

Shaded regions are where each atom is true. Atom labels $A_1, A_2, \ldots$ match the list on the left. Drag the dot to pick a model. The plane shown is $[-3, 3]^2$.

Reference: Albarghouthi, Introduction to Neural Network Verification, Ch 4 (arXiv:2109.10317). Atoms are written in the standard LRA form $\sum_i c_i x_i + b \mathrel{\bowtie} 0$ for $\bowtie \in \{\le, <, \ge, >, =\}$, with $\ge$ and $>$ rewritten via negation as the book recommends.

The UNSAT certificates shown on reveal are tiny non-negative combinations of the contradicting atoms whose sum yields a constant $\le 0$ inequality with positive constant — i.e., Farkas' lemma in miniature. Real LP/SMT solvers produce exactly these certificates as “proof traces.”
