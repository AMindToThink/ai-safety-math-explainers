Zonotope vs interval bounds

NN Verification · Chapter 9 · companion to Albarghouthi (2021) §9.3–§9.5.

Same toy network and draggable $\ell_\infty$ ball as Ch 8, but the abstract pass now carries a zonotope: every quantity is $c_0 + \sum_i c_i \varepsilon_i$ with shared generators $\varepsilon_i \in [-1, 1]$. Affine nodes propagate by linearity (exact); unstable ReLUs add one new generator each via the parallelogram approximation with slope $\lambda = u / (u - l)$. The right-hand panel compares the interval bound, zonotope bound, and true range on $y$, and shows the $(h_1, h_2)$-space picture where path-sharing is preserved by the zonotope but lost by the interval domain.

Reference: Albarghouthi, Introduction to Neural Network Verification, Ch 9 (arXiv:2109.10317). The unstable-ReLU transformer follows §9.4: slope $\lambda = u/(u - l)$, vertical width $\eta = u(1 - \lambda)/2$, one fresh generator per unstable ReLU.

The $(h_1, h_2)$ parallelogram is the projection of the full zonotope (which has up to four generators here) onto its $h_1$ and $h_2$ coefficients. The 2-D shape is a Minkowski sum of line segments — one per generator — so adding a generator always grows it. When both ReLUs are stable, the parallelogram collapses to a line segment because $h_1$ and $h_2$ are both scaled copies of $(\varepsilon \varepsilon_1 \pm \varepsilon \varepsilon_2)$ — see how thin the purple shape becomes for the “single-region” preset.
