Adversarial or certificate?

NN Verification · Chapter 10 · companion to Albarghouthi (2021) §10.3–§10.4. The headline widget.

A robustness spec on the canonical 2-2-1 ReLU toy net says “for every input in the $\ell_\infty$ ball, the output $y$ stays $\le M$.” Two ways to settle it: find an adversary (drag the red marker until $y$ at the marker exceeds $M$) — that refutes the spec — or read the DeepPoly certificate (the polyhedral upper bound on $y$ over the whole ball) — if the certificate is $\le M$, the spec is provably robust. Sometimes neither side wins: the bound is loose, and no attack has been found yet. That's the case where α,β-CROWN's branch-and-bound takes over.

Reference: Albarghouthi, Introduction to Neural Network Verification, Ch 10 (arXiv:2109.10317). The triangle relaxation is the most precise sound convex over-approximation of ReLU on $[l, u]$ — proved by §10's visualisation of the only convex shape touching the three extremal points $(l, 0)$, $(0, 0)$, $(u, u)$. DeepPoly (Singh et al. POPL 2019), CROWN (Zhang et al. NeurIPS 2018), and α,β-CROWN all propagate this relaxation; what differs is how they back-substitute and where they branch.

Real DeepPoly chooses the lower-bound slope adaptively ($\lambda \in \{0, u/(u-l)\}$, picked to minimise the resulting interval width). This widget uses the fixed-$\lambda = u/(u-l)$ upper bound only — that is the quantity that participates in upper-bound certificates for $y \le M$ specs. The lower-bound choice would matter if you were certifying $y \ge M_-$ instead.
