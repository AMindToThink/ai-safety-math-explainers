The Fisher information matrix $I(w)$ encodes how sharply the likelihood discriminates parameters near $w$. Its entries are $$ I_{j,k}(w) \;=\; \iint \big(\partial_{w_j} \log p(y \mid x, w)\big)\big(\partial_{w_k} \log p(y \mid x, w)\big)\, p(y \mid x, w)\, q(x)\, dx\, dy. $$ At a true parameter $w^{(0)} \in W_0$ the Fisher info equals the Hessian of the loss landscape, $I(w^{(0)}) = H_K(w^{(0)})$ — a fact you can compute directly here.

Definition (Carroll, after Watanabe): a model class is regular if it is identifiable and $I(w)$ is positive definite for every $w \in W$. Otherwise it is strictly singular: there exists some $w^{(0)} \in W$ with $\det I(w^{(0)}) = 0$. Pick a $K$, pick a true parameter, and predict $\mathrm{rank}(I(w^{(0)}))$ before the widget computes it. The Example 1.2 case is the surprise.

How this widget computes $I(w^{(0)})$. For each preset, $K(w)$ is a polynomial, so the Hessian $H_K$ is hard-coded as an exact symbolic matrix and evaluated at the chosen $w^{(0)}$. The eigenvalues, determinant, and rank are then numerical (with a tolerance of $10^{-9}$ for "is this eigenvalue zero?"). The Fisher level set drawn in 2D is the locus $\{\delta w : \tfrac12 \delta w^\top I(w^{(0)})\, \delta w = c\}$ for a small fixed $c$; for positive-definite $I$ this is an ellipse with semi-axes $\sqrt{2c/\lambda_i}$ along eigendirections, and as $\lambda_i \to 0$ the corresponding semi-axis blows up — the ellipse degenerates to a strip (rank 1) or vanishes into the whole plane (rank 0). That degeneration is exactly the geometric content of "the model has free directions at $w^{(0)}$." The 1-hidden-unit ReLU model uses Carroll thesis Lemma 3.2 to express $K(a, b) = \tfrac{1}{4} a^2 b^2$ under standard-normal inputs and zero truth; that recovers Example 1.2 verbatim, demonstrating that even the simplest ReLU network is strictly singular.
