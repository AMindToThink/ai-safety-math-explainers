In the Bayesian setup of SLT, the posterior $p(w \mid D_n) = \frac{1}{Z_n}\,\varphi(w)\, e^{-n L_n(w)}$ concentrates as $n \to \infty$ on parameters that minimise the empirical loss $L_n(w)$. The infinite-data limit of this loss is the Kullback–Leibler divergence between truth and model, $$ K(w) \;=\; \iint q(y \mid x)\, q(x)\, \log \frac{q(y \mid x)}{p(y \mid x, w)}\, dx\, dy \;\geq\; 0, $$ which we call the loss landscape. Its zero set $$ W_0 \;=\; \{ w \in W : K(w) = 0 \} $$ is the set of true parameters. In regular models $W_0$ is a single point and the Fisher information is non-degenerate there; in singular models $W_0$ can be a curve, an arrangement of curves, or a higher-dimensional variety. That difference is the entire reason classical Bayesian asymptotics break.

How this widget thinks about $W_0$. For each preset, $W_0$ is computed algebraically from the factorisation of $K$ and drawn in red on top of the level-set contours. The contours come from sampling $K$ on an 80×80 grid and running marching squares (D3's d3.contours) at logarithmically-spaced thresholds. The 1D presets switch the plot from a 2D contour to a 1D curve $K(w)$ vs $w$, with the zeros marked. Local Real Log Canonical Threshold (RLCT) values quoted in the reveal text appear in Carroll's DSLT 1 worked examples and are derived in the next widget, dslt1-volume-scaling-rlct.
