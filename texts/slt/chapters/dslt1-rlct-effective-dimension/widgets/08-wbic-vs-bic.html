Widget 4 showed that the classical $$ \mathrm{BIC} \;=\; n\, L_n(w^{(0)}) \;+\; \tfrac{d}{2}\,\log n $$ is wrong for singular models — the slope $d/2$ overestimates the complexity. Widgets 5 and 6 defined the RLCT $\lambda \leq d/2$ as the right replacement. Widget 7 showed that any analytic $K$ can be put in normal-crossing form, so $\lambda$ is well-defined for any model. This widget is the chapter's payoff: the Widely Applicable Bayesian Information Criterion $$ \mathrm{WBIC} \;=\; n\, L_n(w^{(0)}) \;+\; \lambda\, \log n. $$ Pick a $K$, predict which of $\{$BIC, WBIC, numerical truth$\}$ lie on top of each other in the limit, and watch the receipt match.

How the receipt is computed. The "numerical truth" is $-\log Z_n - n\, L_n(w^{(0)})$ where $Z_n = \int_W e^{-n K(w)}\, dw$ over the unit cube (1D: $[-1, 1]$ at 8001 points; 2D: $[-1, 1]^2$ at $401 \times 401$, with log-sum-exp for stability). Since each preset has $L_n(w^{(0)}) = K(w^{(0)}) = 0$ (the toy K's all have minimum 0), the second term is zero and we plot $-\log Z_n$ directly. The BIC and WBIC are theoretical predictions: $\tfrac{d}{2} \log n$ and $\lambda \log n$ respectively. RLCTs $\lambda$ for the presets come from widgets 5 and 6 in closed form. For $K = w_1^2 w_2^2$ specifically, the multiplicity-2 logarithmic correction $\log(1/\varepsilon)$ shows up as a slow upward drift of the truth line above the WBIC line; that's expected, and the WBIC slope is still the dominant exponent.
