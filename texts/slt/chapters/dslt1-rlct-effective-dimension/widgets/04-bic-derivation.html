Where the BIC derivation breaks for singular models

The Bayesian Information Criterion says the asymptotic free energy of a $d$-parameter model is $$ F_n \;=\; -\log Z_n \;\approx\; n\, L_n(w^{(0)}) \;+\; \tfrac{d}{2} \log n. $$ But every step of its derivation relies, somewhere, on $I(w^{(0)})$ being non-degenerate — on the model being regular. Identifying where the assumption is invoked is the moment that motivates singular learning theory.

Below is the derivation, lifted from Carroll 2023. Predict which step is the first to require $\det I(w^{(0)}) \neq 0$. Then the widget's lower panel computes $-\log Z_n$ numerically for $K = w^2$ (regular) and $K = w^4$ (singular) and overlays the BIC's prediction, so you can see exactly how much it lies for the singular case.

Step 1.
Taylor-expand the empirical NLL $L_n(w) = -\tfrac{1}{n} \sum_i \log p(y_i \mid x_i, w)$ around a true parameter $w^{(0)} \in W_0$: $$ L_n(w) \;=\; L_n(w^{(0)}) \;+\; (w - w^{(0)})^\top \nabla L_n\big|_{w^{(0)}} \;+\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,(w - w^{(0)})^\top J(w^{(0)})\, (w - w^{(0)}) \;+\; O\!\left(\|w - w^{(0)}\|^3\right). $$
Step 2.
$w^{(0)}$ is a minimum, so $\nabla L_n\big|_{w^{(0)}} = 0$. As $n \to \infty$, the Hessian of $L_n$ converges to the Fisher information: $$ J(w^{(0)}) \;=\; \partial^2_{w} L_n(w)\big|_{w^{(0)}} \;\xrightarrow{n \to \infty}\; \partial^2_{w} K(w)\big|_{w^{(0)}} \;=\; I(w^{(0)}). $$
Step 3.
Substitute the truncated Taylor expansion into the partition function $Z_n = \int_W e^{-n L_n(w)}\, \varphi(w)\, dw$: $$ Z_n \;\approx\; e^{-n L_n(w^{(0)})} \int_W \exp\!\Big(-\tfrac{n}{2} (w - w^{(0)})^\top I(w^{(0)})\, (w - w^{(0)})\Big)\, \varphi(w)\, dw. $$
Step 4. crux
If $I(w^{(0)})$ is non-degenerate, the Gaussian integral evaluates explicitly: $$ \int_{\mathbb{R}^d} \exp\!\Big(-\tfrac{n}{2} u^\top I(w^{(0)})\, u\Big)\, du \;=\; \frac{(2\pi)^{d/2}}{n^{d/2}\,\sqrt{\det I(w^{(0)})}}. $$ The remaining $\varphi(w) \approx \varphi(w^{(0)})$ contribution rides along at $O(1)$.
Step 5.
Read off $F_n = -\log Z_n$ from the result of step 4: $$ F_n \;=\; n\, L_n(w^{(0)}) \;+\; \tfrac{d}{2}\log n \;-\; \log \varphi(w^{(0)}) \;-\; \tfrac{d}{2}\log 2\pi \;+\; \tfrac{1}{2}\log \det I(w^{(0)}). $$
Step 6.
Drop $O(1)$ terms and call what's left the BIC: $$ \mathrm{BIC} \;=\; n\, L_n(w^{(0)}) \;+\; \tfrac{d}{2}\log n. $$

The numerical receipt: BIC slope vs reality

For $K(w) = w^2$ and $K(w) = w^4$ (both with $w^{(0)} = 0$, both 1D so $d = 1$), the partition function $Z_n = \int_{-1}^{1} e^{-n K(w)}\, dw$ is computed numerically below. The BIC prediction $-\log Z_n \approx \tfrac{d}{2}\log n = \tfrac{1}{2}\log n$ is plotted as a dashed line. Regular (slope ½) sits on it; singular (slope ¼) peels away.

$-\log Z_n$ for $K = w^2$ (regular; truth slope $1/2$) $-\log Z_n$ for $K = w^4$ (singular; truth slope $1/4 = $ RLCT $\lambda$) BIC prediction $\tfrac{1}{2} \log n$

How the receipt is computed. $Z_n^{(K)} = \int_{-1}^{1} e^{-n K(w)}\, dw$ via 8001-point trapezoid (uniform prior on $[-1,1]$). Plotted as $-\log Z_n - n\, L_n(0)$ vs $\log n$ on a log-spaced grid of $n \in \{10, \ldots, 10^4\}$. The exact asymptotics ($\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-n w^2}\, dw = \sqrt{\pi/n}$ and $\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-n w^4}\, dw = \tfrac{1}{2}\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{4})\, n^{-1/4}$) give slopes $\tfrac{1}{2}$ and $\tfrac{1}{4}$ respectively — matching $\lambda = d/2$ for the regular case and Watanabe's RLCT $\lambda = 1/4$ for $K = w^{2k}$ with $k = 2$.
