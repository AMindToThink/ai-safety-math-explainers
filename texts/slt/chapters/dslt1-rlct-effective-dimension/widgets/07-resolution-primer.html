In widget 6 we computed RLCTs for $K$ already in normal-crossing form. Hironaka's resolution of singularities says we don't have to be that lucky: for any analytic $K \geq 0$, there is a smooth manifold $M$ and a real-analytic map $g \colon M \to W$ such that after pulling back along $g$, $K(g(u))$ is normal-crossing in every local chart: $$ K(g(u)) \;=\; u_1^{2 k_1} \cdots u_d^{2 k_d}, \qquad |g'(u)| \;=\; |u_1^{h_1} \cdots u_d^{h_d}|\, \phi(u),\;\;\phi > 0. $$ The RLCT is then $\lambda = \min_\alpha \min_j (h_j + 1)/(2 k_j)$ over local charts $M_\alpha$. The whole point of the resolution is to make widget 6's calculation universal.

This widget shows the simplest blow-up: replace the origin in $\mathbb{R}^2$ with a $\mathbb{P}^1$ of directions, parameterised in one chart by $(x, y) = (u, u v)$. The Jacobian is $u$, so $|g'| = |u|$ — this is the $h_1 = 1$ that appears in the formula. For each $K$ below, predict whether one blow-up in this chart already gives normal-crossing form near the origin.

Notation. The "blow-up at the origin" of $\mathbb{R}^2$ is a 2-manifold $M$ that comes with two charts; this widget shows only the first chart, $(x, y) = (u, u v)$, whose Jacobian is $u$. The second chart, $(x, y) = (u v, v)$, has Jacobian $v$, and "true" Hironaka resolution requires you to check both charts. A $K$ that's already normal-crossing in chart 1 may need additional blow-ups in chart 2 (cusp $K = (x^3 - y^2)^2$ is the canonical example). The active check sticks to chart 1 to keep the moving parts down. In a real computation, $\lambda$ is determined by the chart that gives the smallest $(h_j + 1)/(2 k_j)$; the widget's reveals quote local exponents in chart 1 only and flag when chart 2 changes the answer. Contour plots are computed on a $90 \times 90$ grid via d3.contours; the colour ramp is shared between original and pulled-back panels so you can compare visually.
