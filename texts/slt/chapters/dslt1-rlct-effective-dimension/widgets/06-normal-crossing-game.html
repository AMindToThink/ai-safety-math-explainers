Once $K(w)$ is in normal-crossing form, the RLCT stops being an asymptotic and becomes a calculation: $$ K(w) = w_1^{2k_1} \cdots w_d^{2k_d} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \lambda = \min_{j} \frac{1}{2 k_j},\quad m = \#\{ j : 1/(2 k_j) = \lambda \}. $$ The 1D case extends to products $K(w) = (w - c_1)^{2 k_1} \cdots (w - c_J)^{2 k_J}$: each $c_j$ contributes a local RLCT $\lambda_j = 1/(2 k_j)$, and the global RLCT is the smallest. After widget 7's resolution-of-singularities primer, this calculation will work for any analytic $K$ (modulo a Jacobian factor).

How the game scores you. Each round you pick $\lambda$ and $m$ separately; both must be right for the round to count, and you only get one shot per question (after either choice is locked, the buttons disable and the reveal explains the calculation in detail). For 1D $K = (w - c_1)^{2 k_1} \cdots (w - c_J)^{2 k_J}$ the local RLCT at $w = c_j$ is $\lambda_j = 1/(2 k_j)$, the global is $\lambda = \min_j \lambda_j$, and the multiplicity is the number of $c_j$'s that achieve that minimum. For multidim normal-crossing $K = w_1^{2 k_1} \cdots w_d^{2 k_d}$, $\lambda = \min_j 1/(2 k_j)$ and $m$ is the number of $k_j$ tied for the minimum (i.e. for the maximum exponent). Carroll's Example 1.4 and Example 1.5 are the templates for rounds 2 and 5.
